Can foreigners buy property in Ras Al Khaimah? (2026 guide)

By the RFR Advisory team · Published 24 November 2026. Freehold areas and fees as published by Ras Al Khaimah Municipality (Lands & Properties Sector), checked on 14 November 2026.

Short answer

Yes. Foreigners can buy freehold property in Ras Al Khaimah’s designated freehold areas: Al Hamra Village, Mina Al Arab, Al Marjan Island, RAK Central, Julphar Towers, Al Maareedh (plots) and the upcoming Beach District. You own it outright, on a title deed. UAE and GCC nationals can buy anywhere in the Emirate. The buyer pays a 2% registration fee.

( 02 ) Ownership

What freehold means for you.

Freehold means full ownership in your own name, recorded on a title deed.

Check the exact unit

Before you pay a deposit, confirm that the exact plot or building sits inside a freehold area. In Downtown RAK, freehold is limited to designated projects such as Julphar Towers and RAK Central; outside the freehold areas, ask in writing what ownership is on offer.

Do you need to be in the UAE?

Not necessarily. Foreign nationals can buy freehold whether they are UAE residents or live overseas. We run video viewings for overseas buyers, and a power of attorney can be used if someone acts for you at the transfer. Once you own it, we can manage the property for you.

Is there tax on property in the UAE?

There is no income tax or capital gains tax on property in the UAE. The registration fees below still apply when you buy.

( 03 ) Costs

What it costs to buy.

The main government cost is the registration fee: 2% of the property’s market value, paid by the buyer. The seller pays a further 2%. Two small issuance fees and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee are added on top.

( 04 ) The process

How foreigners buy property in Ras Al Khaimah, step by step.

Seven steps from budget to title deed.

The seven steps

  1. Set your budget and goal. Decide whether the property is to live in, to rent out or for a Golden Visa, and include the 2% registration fee in the budget.
  2. Shortlist an area. Match the goal to the area: yield in Downtown RAK or Al Hamra Village, space in Mina Al Arab, resort-led off-plan on Al Marjan Island. Compare the four areas.
  3. View the property. In person, or on video if you are abroad.
  4. Reserve and sign. Agree the price and sign a memorandum of understanding (MOU) for a resale, or the developer’s sale and purchase agreement (SPA) for off-plan.
  5. Get the developer NOC. In freehold areas the developer issues a no-objection certificate before the transfer.
  6. Transfer and register. Complete the transfer at RAK Municipality, pay the registration fee and receive the title deed.
  7. Take handover, or let it out. Move in, or let our property management team find and manage tenants.

Documents for a resale purchase

  • Emirates ID or passport
  • A no-objection certificate (NOC) from the developer, for freehold areas
  • The seller’s original title deed
  • A power of attorney, if an agent acts for either party

For an off-plan unit you sign the developer’s SPA and pay against its schedule.

( 05 ) Off-plan

Can foreigners buy off-plan?

Yes. Al Marjan Island is freehold, so foreign buyers can own off-plan units there outright, the same as ready homes.

( 06 ) Residency

Does buying property give foreigners a Golden Visa?

It can. Property worth AED 2M or more can qualify you for a UAE Golden Visa, subject to the federal ICP rules at the time you apply.

5 or 10 years?

Official pages differ on the term: ICP and RAKEZ describe 10 years, while the u.ae portal lists 5 years for real estate investments. The permit ICP issues states its own validity, so confirm the term with ICP. RAKEZ states that the applicant must be inside the UAE when submitting the request.

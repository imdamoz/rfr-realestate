( Checklist )

What a good RAK agent should do for you.

Whichever agency you use, these are the things to ask for. None of them is specific to us; use the list to compare agents side by side.

  1. Show you their licence and registration. Ask for the agency’s licence and the individual agent’s registration, and check them with the official Ras Al Khaimah land and property registry before you share documents or pay anything.
  2. Put every fee in writing, up front. Agency commission is agreed with the agent; it is not set by the authorities. Ask what you pay, who pays it, whether VAT is on top and when it falls due. Government fees, such as the registration fee paid to RAK Municipality, are separate: what it costs to buy.
  3. Compare areas for your goal, not theirs. A good agent asks what the home is for (to live in, to let or for a Golden Visa) and compares areas on price, rent and yield before showing you units. The four areas we cover, compared.
  4. Confirm who can own the property. If you are a foreign buyer, the agent should confirm that the exact building or plot sits inside a designated freehold area before you pay a deposit. Can foreigners buy property in Ras Al Khaimah?
  5. Check the developer and the payment plan. For off-plan, ask for the SPA payment schedule in writing, work out how much you pay before handover and ask what happens if the date moves. Off-plan payment plans in Ras Al Khaimah.
  6. Explain the price. Price against recent sales, a realistic rent, the service charge and the risks, including when a launch is one to skip.
  7. See the paperwork through to the title deed. The MOU for a resale or the developer’s SPA for off-plan, the developer NOC, then the transfer and registration. The seven steps to the title deed.
  8. Help at handover. Walk the home with you, or for you, at handover, note any defects for the developer to put right, and stay reachable after the keys.
  9. Work properly with you from abroad. Video viewings, clear written updates and, if someone acts for you at the transfer, a power of attorney.

General guidance, not legal advice. Rules and fees can change; confirm them with the authority before you commit.

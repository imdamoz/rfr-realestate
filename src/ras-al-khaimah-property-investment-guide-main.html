Investing in Ras Al Khaimah property: a 2026 guide for buyers and investors.

By the RFR Advisory team · Updated 22 November 2026. Pulled together from our market note, area guides, off-plan comparison and buyer’s guide for foreigners.

Short answer

Ras Al Khaimah can be a good place to buy property, for the right buyer. Foreigners can buy freehold in designated areas such as Al Marjan Island, Mina Al Arab and Al Hamra Village; there is no income or capital gains tax on UAE property; and property worth AED 2M or more can qualify you for a Golden Visa, subject to ICP rules. Apartments start from about AED 400K in Downtown RAK, and indicative gross yields across the four main areas run from 5.5% to 9%. The buyer pays a 2% registration fee. But 2026 price growth slowed, more than 34,000 new homes are expected by 2030, and nobody knows yet what the Wynn resort, due in 2027, will do to prices.

Al Marjan Island

Four man-made, coral-shaped islands off the Al Hamra coast, where most of Ras Al Khaimah’s branded residences are being built and where the Wynn resort is due to open in 2027. It suits overseas investors, holiday-let owners, resort professionals and second-home buyers from Dubai. Supply is finite by design: the islands are fully master-planned. Al Marjan Island guide · compare on the areas page.

Mina Al Arab

A low-rise waterfront community of villas, townhouses and lagoon-front apartments, built around lagoons and protected mangroves. It suits families relocating to RAK, long-term residents and buyers who want water without the resort crowds. The Ritz-Carlton Residences and Porto Playa are the next phase. Mina Al Arab area guide.

Al Hamra Village

The Emirate’s original master community, with an 18-hole championship golf course, a marina and yacht club, a mall and the deepest rental market in RAK. It is the closest established community to Al Marjan Island, so it’s where the resort staff arriving in 2027 will rent. Al Hamra Village area guide.

Downtown RAK

The working city around Al Nakheel, Julphar and the corniche, with the shortest commutes to RAKEZ, the hospitals and government, and the lowest entry price of the four. Less glamour, more cash flow. Freehold here is limited to designated projects such as Julphar Towers and RAK Central. Downtown RAK area guide.

( 03 ) Off-plan or ready

Pay in stages, or move in now.

Off-plan means paying in instalments for a home that isn’t finished yet, usually at a lower entry price. A ready home costs more upfront, but you can view it and move into it straight away.

Buying off-plan

You sign for a home before it’s finished and decide from renders, floor plans, virtual tours and a visit to the site. Launch prices usually undercut finished homes, most launches are on the waterfront by the resorts, and you pay in instalments as it’s built.

Our current book carries nine off-plan launches on Al Marjan Island, with developer entry prices from AED 920,000 to AED 4.06M, on plans from 50/50 to 80/20 (for example 60/40: 60% paid during construction and 40% on or after handover). Handovers run from Q4 2026 to 2028.

Off-plan payment plans in Ras Al Khaimah

Plans run from 50/50 to 80/20, meaning 50% to 80% is paid during construction and the rest on or after handover. That structure matters more than the headline price: a 60/40 plan on a Q4 2028 handover means most of your capital stays with you until the resort is already open.

Pick the plan and the handover date before the brand. A Q4 2026 unit rents a year earlier; a 2028 unit keeps your cash longer. Manta Bay, Sora Beach Residences and Masa Residence are the three launches in our book handing over in Q4 2026.

What to watch with off-plan

Handover dates can slip, so ask for the SPA payment schedule, work out how much you pay before handover and ask what happens if the date moves. Starting prices are the lowest entry point; floor, view and the payment plan chosen all move the final figure. Check the developer’s track record, the payment plan and the exact plot before you sign. ( 04 ) Ownership

Can foreigners buy property in Ras Al Khaimah?

Yes, in the Emirate’s designated freehold areas. Freehold areas are as published by Ras Al Khaimah Municipality (Lands & Properties Sector), checked on 14 November 2026.

Where foreigners can own freehold

Ras Al Khaimah Municipality lists the freehold areas as Al Hamra Village, Mina Al Arab, Al Marjan Island, RAK Central, Julphar Towers, Al Maareedh (plots) and the upcoming Beach District. UAE and GCC nationals can buy anywhere in the Emirate.

Freehold means full ownership in your own name, recorded on a title deed. Before you pay a deposit, confirm that the exact plot or building sits inside a freehold area; in Downtown RAK, freehold is limited to designated projects such as Julphar Towers and RAK Central.

Buying from abroad, and tax

You don’t necessarily need to be in the UAE to buy. We run video viewings for overseas buyers, and a power of attorney can be used if someone acts for you at the transfer.

There is no income tax or capital gains tax on property in the UAE. The registration fees still apply when you buy. Full detail in our guide to buying property in Ras Al Khaimah as a foreigner.

( 05 ) The process

How to buy property in Ras Al Khaimah, step by step.

Seven steps from budget to title deed, as set out in our buyer’s guide for foreigners.

The seven steps

  1. Set your budget and goal. Decide whether the property is to live in, to rent out or for a Golden Visa, and include the 2% registration fee in the budget.
  2. Shortlist an area. Match the goal to the area: yield in Downtown RAK or Al Hamra Village, space in Mina Al Arab, resort-led off-plan on Al Marjan Island. Compare the four areas.
  3. View the property. In person, or on video if you are abroad.
  4. Reserve and sign. Agree the price and sign a memorandum of understanding (MOU) for a resale, or the developer’s sale and purchase agreement (SPA) for off-plan.
  5. Get the developer NOC. In freehold areas the developer issues a no-objection certificate before the transfer.
  6. Transfer and register. Complete the transfer at RAK Municipality, pay the registration fee and receive the title deed.
  7. Take handover, or let it out. Move in, or let our property management team find and manage tenants.

Documents for a resale purchase

  • Emirates ID or passport
  • A no-objection certificate (NOC) from the developer, for freehold areas
  • The seller’s original title deed
  • A power of attorney, if an agent acts for either party

For an off-plan unit you sign the developer’s SPA and pay against its schedule.

( 06 ) Costs

What it costs to buy.

The main government cost is the registration fee: 2% of the property’s market value, paid by the buyer. The seller pays a further 2%. Two small issuance fees and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee are added on top.

( 07 ) Residency

Does buying property in Ras Al Khaimah give you a Golden Visa?

It can. Property worth AED 2M or more can qualify you for a UAE Golden Visa, subject to the federal ICP rules at the time you apply.

5 or 10 years?

Official pages differ on the term: ICP and RAKEZ describe 10 years, while the u.ae portal lists 5 years for real estate investments. RAKEZ states that the applicant must be inside the UAE when submitting the request.

If the visa is the goal

( 08 ) Checklist

What to check before you buy.

Start with one question: is the property to live in, to rent out or for a Golden Visa? Buyers with a clear answer, a budget that includes the fees, and the patience to check the developer, the payment plan and the handover date are the ones this market suits.

Seven checks

  1. Confirm the rules. Rules and fees can change. Confirm them with the authority, or ask us, before you commit.
  2. Check the freehold status of the exact unit. Before you pay a deposit, confirm that the plot or building sits inside a designated freehold area.
  3. Check the developer, the plan and the plot. Look at the developer’s track record, the payment plan and the exact plot before you sign.
  4. Get the payment schedule in writing. For off-plan, ask for the SPA schedule, work out how much you pay before handover, and ask what happens if the handover date slips.
  5. Ask for the service charge. Running costs vary from building to building. Get the estimate in writing and include it in any yield sum.
  6. Look for evidence of rental demand. Ask for rents achieved in completed buildings nearby, not a projected yield, and check how much competing supply hands over in the same quarter.
  7. Budget the total cost. Add the 2% registration fee, AED 200 for plan issuance, AED 200 for the title deed, agency commission, furnishing and any developer charges.

Mina Al Arab, Ras Al Khaimah

Lagoons, mangroves and the quiet side of the coast.

BY THE RFR ADVISORY TEAM · PUBLISHED 15 NOVEMBER 2026

Mina Al Arab is a low-rise waterfront community in Ras Al Khaimah, built around lagoons and protected mangroves and anchored by the Anantara and InterContinental resorts. It is one of the Emirate’s designated freehold areas, so foreign buyers can own property there.

This guide brings together what we publish about Mina Al Arab: the homes, indicative prices, rents and gross yield, who lives there, what is on the doorstep and how buying works. For the same figures at a glance, see our Mina Al Arab area page.

( 01 ) WHAT IT IS

What is Mina Al Arab?

Mina Al Arab is a low-rise waterfront community built around lagoons and protected mangroves, anchored by the Anantara and InterContinental resorts. The homes are villas, townhouses and lagoon-front apartments in an established, family-led neighbourhood.

Ras Al Khaimah Municipality lists Mina Al Arab among the Emirate’s designated freehold areas, alongside Al Hamra Village, Al Marjan Island, RAK Central, Julphar Towers, Al Maareedh (plots) and the upcoming Beach District. As our buying guide notes, UAE and GCC nationals can buy anywhere in the Emirate, and before you pay a deposit you should confirm that the exact plot or building sits inside a freehold area.

( 05 ) BUYING HERE

How buying works in Mina Al Arab

The rules and fees for Mina Al Arab are the same as for Ras Al Khaimah’s other freehold areas. The figures below are reused from our guide to buying property in Ras Al Khaimah, which cites Ras Al Khaimah Municipality’s published service information.

  • Registration fee: 2% of the property’s market value, paid by the buyer. The seller pays a further 2%.
  • Issuance fees: AED 200 for plan issuance and AED 200 for the title deed.
  • Mortgage registration: 0.1% of the loan, only where there is a mortgage.
  • Agency commission and developer charges: separate, and not set by the authorities.

The buying guide’s step-by-step section suggests matching your goal to the area, and lists “space in Mina Al Arab” alongside yield in Downtown RAK or Al Hamra Village and resort-led off-plan on Al Marjan Island. It also covers documents for a resale, the developer NOC and the transfer at RAK Municipality. If you are abroad, RFR runs video viewings for overseas buyers.

Property worth AED 2M or more can qualify you for a UAE Golden Visa, subject to the federal ICP rules at the time you apply. If the visa is part of the plan, read Golden Visa via RAK property first, because it shapes the budget and the choice of unit.

( 06 ) FAQ

Common questions

Can foreigners buy property in Mina Al Arab?

Yes. Mina Al Arab is one of Ras Al Khaimah’s designated freehold areas, so foreign buyers can own property there. RAK Municipality lists the freehold areas as Al Hamra Village, Mina Al Arab, Al Marjan Island, RAK Central, Julphar Towers, Al Maareedh (plots) and the upcoming Beach District.

How much does property cost in Mina Al Arab?

Apartments start from about AED 800K, and villas sit at about AED 2.5–6M. These ranges are indicative, based on RFR listings and public launch data.

How much is rent in Mina Al Arab?

A three-bedroom villa rents for about AED 85–140K a year and a two-bedroom apartment for about AED 60–95K a year. Figures are indicative; exact rents for any unit are available on request.

What rental yield can I expect in Mina Al Arab?

About 5.5–7% gross, as an indicative range. Ask RFR for the figures on a specific unit.

Who lives in Mina Al Arab and what is nearby?

Families relocating to RAK, long-term residents, and buyers who want water without the resort crowds. The Anantara and InterContinental resorts, the mangrove beach and boardwalks are on the doorstep, RAK Academy is about 10 minutes away, RAK city centre about 15 minutes and RAK Airport about 30 minutes.

What are the government fees when you buy in Mina Al Arab?

The same as elsewhere in Ras Al Khaimah, as set out in our buying guide: the buyer pays a registration fee of 2% of the market value, plus AED 200 for plan issuance and AED 200 for the title deed. If you use a mortgage, its registration costs 0.1% of the loan. Agency commission and developer charges are separate.
